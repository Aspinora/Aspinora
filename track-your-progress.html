Studying without tracking feels like running in the dark. Here's how to see exactly where you stand — and stay motivated because of it.

Why tracking matters

It's easy to feel like you're "not doing enough," even when you're making real progress — because effort is invisible unless you record it. Tracking turns vague anxiety into a clear picture: what's improving, what's stuck, and what needs more attention.

It also protects your motivation. On hard days, looking back at a month of steady progress can carry you through — a feeling you'll never get if nothing is written down.

Tips that actually help

①

Log right after studying

Memory fades fast — record what you did while it's still fresh.

②

Watch trends, not single days

One bad day means nothing. A week of bad days means something.

③

Celebrate small wins

A 5% jump in accuracy is real progress — acknowledge it.

④

Revisit weak areas weekly

Tracking is only useful if you act on what it shows you.
