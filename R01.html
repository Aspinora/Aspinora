Electoral Reforms in India

INTRODUCTION

India's electoral system, whilst demonstrating unparalleled resilience and scale, confronts multifaceted crises undermining democratic legitimacy, institutional integrity, and inclusive participation. With 970+ million voters (2024 Lok Sabha) across 543 constituencies, India orchestrates the world's largest democratic exercise; yet voter turnout volatility (65.79% in 2024), electoral malpractices (voter bribery affecting one-fifth of electorate in 15-20 years), unregulated campaign financing (exceeding statutory caps with impunity), and deepfake manipulation precipitate systemic governance deficits. The Election Commission of India's (ECI) 23 new initiatives (2025)—spanning ECINET digital platform, remote voting pilots, electoral roll accuracy, model code enforcement—exemplify institutional responsiveness. This analysis examines electoral reforms addressing procedural transparency, participatory inclusion, technological integrity, and enforcement mechanisms essential for preserving democratic constitutionalism in India's Viksit Bharat 2047 vision.

CORE DIMENSIONS
1. CAMPAIGN FINANCE REGULATION & POLITICAL FUNDING TRANSPARENCY
  • Electoral Bonds controversy: Opacity exacerbating "money-politics nexus"; ₹6,000+ crore corporate funding (2019-2024) concentrated among ruling coalitions
  • Campaign spending violations endemic; spending caps routinely exceeded by 50-100%; enforcement mechanisms impotent and delayed
  • Proposed reforms: Mandatory disclosure of political funding sources; statutory Model Code of Conduct (MCC) with enhanced ECI enforcement powers; caps on individual/corporate donations
2. VOTER PARTICIPATION & ACCESSIBILITY ENHANCEMENT
  • 65.79% voter turnout (2024); urban participation lagging rural; youth apathy reducing 18-29 demographic engagement
  • ECI initiatives: Multi-Constituency Remote Voting Machines (RVMs) managing up to 72 constituencies from single booth; mobile polling station facilities in high-rises; voter turnout capped at 1,200 per station
  • SVEEP programme expansion (Systematic Voters' Education): Targeting 75% turnout in Lok Sabha; gender gap reduction; marginalised section inclusion
3. ELECTORAL MALPRACTICES & DEEPFAKE MITIGATION
  • Voter bribery endemic: One-fifth electorate affected over 15-20 years; booth capturing, coercion undermining secret ballot sanctity
  • AI-generated deepfakes proliferating; misinformation campaigns virally amplified; ECI deploying AI-powered hate speech detection on social media
  • Enforcement reforms: Star campaigner status revocation for Model Code violations; party recognition suspension mechanisms; enhanced penalties for malicious disinformation
4. ELECTORAL ROLL ACCURACY & VOTER REGISTRATION MODERNIZATION
  • Bogus voter menace persisting; Special Intensive Revision (SIR) affecting millions amid transparency concerns
  • ECINET platform (2025): Digitised acquisition of death registrations; unique EPIC number mechanism eliminating duplicate entries; electronic electoral roll upgradation
  • Voter registration expansion: Electronic death registry integration; real-time duplicate detection; gender parity enhancement in enrolment
5. ECI INSTITUTIONAL AUTONOMY & CONSTITUTIONAL STRENGTHENING
  • Election Commission (Conduct of Election and Election Matters) Act, 2023 replacing 1991 Act; enhanced Commissioner tenure security and qualification reforms
  • Financial autonomy: ECI budget charged upon Consolidated Fund of India (akin constitutional bodies); preventing political budgetary manipulation
  • Statutory MCC backing; enhanced power to revoke party recognition; increased administrative authority strengthening enforcement credibility

CONCLUSION
Electoral reforms represent India's constitutional commitment to preserving democracy amidst evolving challenges. The ECI's 23 initiatives (2025)—ECINET platform, remote voting, electoral roll digitisation, enforcement strengthening—signal institutional responsiveness to 970+ million voters' aspirations. Yet systemic deficits persist: electoral bond opacity, voter bribery affecting one-fifth electorate, deepfake proliferation, turnout volatility. India's Lok Sabha 2024 (65.79% turnout) demonstrated resilience but unmasked participation gaps requiring urgent remediation. Transformative reforms demand transparent campaign financing, statutory MCC with enforcement teeth, technology-driven malpractice prevention, and ECI institutional autonomy. The 2023 Election Commission Act's constitutional anchoring provides foundational legitimacy; operationalising its provisions whilst implementing participatory reforms offers India a pathway toward electoral democracy where 970 million citizens confidently exercise franchise in free, fair, and technologically-resilient elections serving democratic constitutionalism's highest ideals in pursuit of Viksit Bharat 2047.
